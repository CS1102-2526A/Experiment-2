Under the same conditions as Experiment 1, we are going to observe the acid-base reaction between hydrochloric acid (HCl) and sodium hydroxide (NaOH). When HCl and NaOH are combined, these two chemicals react in a neutralisation reaction and form sodium chloride (NaCl), which is commonly defined as table salt, and water (H₂O). The chemical equation is:

HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O

Using Universal indicator

Universal Indicator is a pH indicator made from a mixture of several chemical solutions. There are two common universal indicators: Universal Indicator Solution and Universal Indicator Paper.

It is different from the litmus paper we have introduced in Experiment 1. Universal Indicator not only can find out whether the solution is an acid, an alkali or neutral, but also can determine the pH value (pH 0 to pH 14) of the solution by comparing it with the pH colour chart.

(Source: Compound Interest https://www.compoundchem.com/2014/04/04/the-colours-chemistry-of-ph-indicators/)


Simulation

Now, we can observe a neutralization reaction of HCl and NaOH with Universal Indicator by a simple animation.

Step 1: Add the universal indicator solution to the same amount of HCl and NaOH respectively.

Step 2: Mix the universal indicator solution with HCl, and the colour of HCl changes from transparent to pink, indicating that HCl is an acidic solution with a pH value of 2.

Step 3: Mix the universal indicator solution with NaOH, and the colour of NaOH changes from transparent to purple, indicating that NaOH is an acidic solution with a pH value of 13.

Step 4: Mix the two mixtures, and it becomes a green solution, which is a neutral solution (pH value of 7) after comparing it with the pH colour chart.
